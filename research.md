# GEB Lab research journal

## Concepts discovered
- Self-reference as structural pattern across logic, art, and mind
- Recursion as a way to instantiate complexity from simple rules
- Meaning as a relationship between syntax, context, and interpretation
- Musical variation as a formal transformation system
- Strange loops as a bridge between hierarchy and feedback

## Visualization ideas considered
- Concept map of shared structures
- Formal rule simulator for the MU puzzle
- Recursive loop sculpture for levels-of-description
- Music engine built from transformation of a motif

## Experiments
1. Built a concept map that allows clicking different nodes to reveal how each concept relates to the others.
2. Implemented a live MU-puzzle toy to show how tiny rules produce large conceptual tension.
3. Built a strange-loop visualizer where depth controls the sense of nested description.
4. Added a Bach-like motif engine that transforms a melody through inversion and augmentation.

## Failed experiments
- A fully 3D scene felt decorative rather than explanatory.
- A large chapter-by-chapter app become linear and static, which weakened the book’s core idea of structures crossing domains.

## Surprising connections
- Musical variation, recursive rule systems, and self-reference all share a common structure: a pattern remains recognizable while being transformed.
- The strongest insights come not from direct chapter summaries but from seeing the same principle in different media.

## New representations
- A "conceptual map" where nodes indicate patterns and edges imply relationship rather than hierarchy.
- A formal tool that enacts the logic of a rule system instead of merely describing it.
- A recursive visualizer that makes levels-of-description physically visible.

## Open questions
- How far can a computational representation push beyond a simple analogy without becoming a misleading abstraction?
- Which ideas in GEB resist visual or auditory simulation most strongly, and why?
- Could an even deeper interface treat the book as a navigable system of transformations rather than discrete topics?
